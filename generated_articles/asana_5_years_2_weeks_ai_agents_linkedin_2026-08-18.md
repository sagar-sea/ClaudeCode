# Asana: 5 Years of Engineering in 2 Weeks With AI Agents

**Created:** 2026-08-18
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-18 20:53
**Format:** LinkedIn Post

---

5 years. $6 million. On the backlog forever.

Asana cleared it in 2 weeks for $12,000.

Here's what actually happened — and what made it work.

**The problem no one wanted to touch:**
Enzyme, their legacy frontend testing framework, had gone unmaintained. Every attempt to modernize the stack hit it like a wall. The standard answer was: "We'll get to it eventually." Eventually was a 5-year roadmap item.

**How AI made this tractable:**
OpenAI Codex agents didn't just write code faster. They changed the shape of the problem.

- Four agents ran in parallel, each working in its own isolated copy of the codebase
- They started from a five-sentence prompt — not a detailed spec
- A single engineer reviewed proposed changes and checked progress twice a day

The human didn't disappear. The human's job shifted: from writing code to directing and approving it.

**What 1.5 weeks of agent work covered:**
The migration that would've needed a dedicated team across years was done. Enzyme removed. Stack unlocked.

The cost breakdown matters here. The $6M estimate was almost entirely engineering hours — salaries, coordination overhead, context-switching costs. The $12K was model and infrastructure. Same outcome, different cost structure entirely.

**The real insight isn't the speed.**

It's that this project existed on the backlog because the math never justified it. $6M to clean up a test framework? You defer that indefinitely. $12K? You do it this sprint.

AI didn't make engineers faster at this task. It made the task fundable for the first time.

As Asana's CTO Amritansh Raghav put it: *"Not every years-long project will collapse into weeks. But agents can give engineers more room for craft — and make once-impossible work worth attempting."*

The backlog you've been deferring? Some of it just became affordable.
