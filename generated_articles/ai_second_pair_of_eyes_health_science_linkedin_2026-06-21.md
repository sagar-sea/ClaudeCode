# AI as the Second Pair of Eyes in Health and Science

**Created:** 2026-06-21
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-21 00:33
**Format:** LinkedIn Post

---

I think we are entering a quieter, more practical phase of AI.

Not the phase where AI writes a poem, makes an image, or answers trivia.

The phase where AI becomes a second pair of eyes on problems humans already care deeply about.

This week, OpenAI shared a rare-disease study with Boston Children's Hospital and Harvard where an AI reasoning workflow reanalyzed 376 previously unsolved cases.

It did not diagnose patients.
It did something more realistic: it surfaced evidence-linked leads for clinicians to review.

After expert review, additional testing, and clinical confirmation, physicians established 18 diagnoses. That is a 4.8% additional yield in cases that had already gone through specialist review.

In another update, OpenAI connected GPT-5.4 to Molecule.one's Maria lab system. The AI helped design experiments for a challenging medicinal chemistry reaction, improving measured yields for 88% of tested boronic acids and 83% of tested sulfonamides.

The lesson for me is simple:

AI's biggest near-term value may not be replacing experts.

It may be helping experts revisit old evidence, connect scattered knowledge, and ask better next questions.

That is useful in medicine.
That is useful in science.
And honestly, that is useful in almost every serious field.
